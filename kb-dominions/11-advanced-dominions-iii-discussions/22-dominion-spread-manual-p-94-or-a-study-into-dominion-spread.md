-> vfb:
#1: Dom spread from temples reduces the number of enemy candles in the province it plops into if it makes a successful check against the existing enemy dominion.

#2: Preaching doesn't really spread dominion, it has a chance based on priest level to increase dominion in the province the priest is in. But if you preach to max, then further dominion spread will flow out of the province. If a Pythium dom spread plops onto your province and passes the check against your existing dominion, you lose a candle. If Aby dom spread plops onto your province and passes the check against your existing dominion, you lose another candle. I don't know which nations spread first.

#3: Max dominion is for dominion spread only. You can have higher than max if you preach, or get a random dominion event, or if you lose some temples.

-> Endoperez:
Most of these answers aren't that important. Instead of formulas, you need to know how to use dominion and what you should be careful about.

Higher dominion is better, but dominion of 4-5 is enough. Having too low max dominion score can be dangerous.

Province with a dominion value of only 1 or 2 will most likely not be under the full scales you chose, which can be important in case of rich farmlands and income-increasing scales.

If you have significantly lower dominion than your opponent, investing (and protecting!) temples and/or priests would be a good idea.

Under negative dominion, positive scales won't help you even if it was your dominion that originally brough those scales there.

Nations who need a spesific temperature need higher dominion, e.g. EA Abysia loses lots of income in Cold 1 province.

-> capnq:
The analogy to a liquid is quite helpful in understanding its behavior. When one "container" (province) is "full", it "spills over" into an adjacent one. If that province is "full", it continues to "overflow" until it reaches a province that isn't "full", which is how it ends up "far away" from the temple where it was "poured" into the network of "containers".

Raising your Maximum Dominion makes all of the "containers" "bigger", and enemy Dominion spread slowly empties your "containers".

-> chrispedersen 
But its *wrong*

Dominion does not spill over when its "full". It spreads continuously. 70+% of the time, it will *not* be expressed in the territory that generated the temple check.

For example:
If there is a world of three provinces. In the following example, A & B are temple provinces with dom 1;"-" is a province with neutral dominion.

A-B

Presume 10 initial dominion - so you are generating 2 temple checks.  27% of the time a will receive a dominion increase - same with b.
However, the most likely dominion result after 1 turn would be: 121.

This is not at all what Capn's explanation says, which predicts a 202 result.

So consider a small map with 4 players, say 60 provinces. Lets assume everyone has 15 provinces, that the blood sacker has dom 10, and that the other players have dom 5. We'll further assume each player has 5 temples, and has miraculously maxed their dominion in-territory.

Each defending player produces 5 temple checks per turn generating on average 2.5 candles.

The blood sacker we'll assume has 5 H2 priests with Jade Knives. They're generating 9x5 = 45 temple checks per turn, and each of those checks is a guaranteed candle. Note this requires the tiny investment of 20 slaves per turn.

Assuming pretenders and prophets are alive, that's another potential 2.5 candles per defender, and another 4 candles for the dom sacker. And the prophet could replace one of those H2 priests for another 2 candles per turn at the cost of 1 slave.

Starting candles:

Maximum turns till map-wide dominion kill: ~20

Note that the blood sacker is making a minimum investment here. Its 21 slaves and 5 priests. He's still perfectly capable of fighting wars. Chances are, if he were pursuing this strategy, he'd have built more temples, so he'd actually have a larger dominion spread advantage. Chances are his opponents wouldn't have built as many temples either, and they'd have fewer starting candles on the map relative to maxed out than the dom sacker would because they have lower candle generation and the dom10 would likely be pushing into their territory.

And none of this stops the blood sacker from outplaying an opponent militarily while he's gearing up for this, since the investment is so minimal.

Provinces/player tends to stay relatively constant with game size, but to win by blood sacrifice you need to get your dominion to cover the map, meaning you have relatively more work to do the larger the map gets.

So here is my understanding of the Dominion spread mechanics.

-------------------------------------------

(disclaimer - all of the following is just my understanding. I make no claims to it being 100% correct, and I'd be more than happy to see any evidence that indicates the contrary on any of this)


To begin with, one important thing to understand is that actually gaining a candle on the map is a two-step process. A successful temple check, and then subsequently gaining a candle is not an automatic guaranteed process, as there are checks involved, and some failed checks can see the candle created from the temple check lost. ie. I am saying that the candle from a successful temple check does not have to go somewhere. (which I am presuming both of you are claiming)

The first step is the temple check. This decides whether or not a 'wandering' candle is created or not. This equation is

10% x (starting) Dominion Strength.

This is (almost) from the manual, and as far as I know it is correct. Albeit with the added (brackets) correction that only your starting dominion strength counts (and not as the manual claims, your current maximum dominion strength). Although if someone wants to point me to testing and evidence that this formula is incorrect, then I'd welcome the chance to learn the actual formula.

If the above equation is correct, then a dom 10 Pretender will always create a 'wandering' candle from every temple check.



The second step is for the 'wandering' candle to try and find a home. Note it does not have a guaranteed home unless it meets a province with neutral dominion. (when the chance of the 'wandering' candle settling there is 100%). The manual claims that a 'wandering' candle has to find a home, but I do not believe this to be the case. (which I will address in 3 below).

So given a successful temple check, the 'wandering' candle created first checks the province it is created in. If this fails, then it moves to a random connecting province to again try to find a home. This 'wandering' candle then has three types of provinces it can encounter. If it encounters......

a..... a province with neutral dominion - It has 100% chance of settling, and you gain a candle. This is from the manual , and I believe it to be correct. (page 93. "If the dominion in the province is neutral, dominion increases to +1 automatically")


b..... a province containing positive dominion - A check is made to decide whether your 'wandering' candle settles or not. The following formula is from the manual, which I believe to be correct.

Chance of number of your candles increasing in a province already containing your candles = 30% - (number of candles currently in the province x 3%).

If a successful check is made, then you gain a candle in that province. If the check fails (in a friendly province), then your 'wandering' candle continues its journey.

This means a province with 1 candle has a 27% chance of increasing if checked by your ''wandering' candle, and at 9 candles you only have a 3% chance of your candles increasing to 10. This is why your dominion always spreads out before it fills up. Since the more candles you have in a province, the more failed checks you will have.

This is what I said, or at least tried to say, in the first paragraph of my post. ie. your dominion strength (not temple check strength) affects your dominion spread. As the stronger your dominion is (ie. the more candles you have) the less chance you have of gaining more candles.


c..... a province containing enemy dominion - A check is made to decide whether your 'wandering' candle settles, and thereby reduces the number of enemy candles by 1. The formula for this in the manual is also correct to the best of my knowledge.

Chance of a 'wandering' candle reducing the number of enemy candles by 1 = 50% + (your starting dominion strength x 5%) - (current number of enemy candles in the province x 5%)

If a successful check is made, then the number of enemy candles is reduced by 1.

Now the crucial difference

If an unsuccessful check is made, the 'wandering' candle is extinguished. (this is the complete opposite of what the manual claims)

So a 'wandering' candle from a 10 dominion Pretender journeying to a province with 10 enemy candles has a 50% chance of reducing that number to 9, and if it journeys to a province with 2 enemy candles, then it has a 90% chance of reducing the number of enemy candles to 1.

This is why a high 9/10 dominion strength Pretender always spreads dominion so well, since it has a very high chance of reducing any low levels of enemy dominion it meets. Whereas a dominion strength 5/6 Pretender, often struggles to reduce enemy dominion.


So if you have 10 positive candles in your province, then the best chance the enemy has to reduce it with temple checks is 50%, which has to be a 'wandering' candle from a Dominion strength 10 Pretender. (This is what I was referring to in the last paragraph of my post.)

---------------------------------------------------

Now I guess the most controversial claim I am making here is that a successful temple check isn't guaranteed to produce a candle, which (I think) is what the manual claims. And that your 'wandering' candles are lost when they encounter a province with enemy dominion, combined with an unsuccessful check. The current theory is that a successful temple check is guaranteed to produce a candle somewhere. I started a game on a linear nine province map, so all the provinces are in a line 1-2-3-4-5-6-7-8-9.

I started a two nation test game with two Dom 10 Pretenders. One starting at province #1, the other at #9. After many turns, as expected, provinces 1-4 and 6-9 filled up with 10 candles of the respective nation. While the middle province #5 obviously saw something different......

Both nations had a capital and a temple, so both were producing the same 3 temple checks. Neither nation had a prophet or god, and neither nation saw any events that changed dominion levels. Both nations were also producing Dominion strength 10 temple checks. ie. all temple checks were guaranteed to produce a 'wandering' candle.

So, if the claim is that each nations 3 'wandering' candles are guaranteed to produce a candle somewhere, then the total number of candles (in this scenario) has to stay the same. As if each candle is guaranteed to gain a candle somewhere, then each candle is being automatically cancelled out by the equivalent 'guaranteed to settle somewhere' candle of the other nation.

But this is not what I observed at all. As the number of candles in province #5 fluctuated quite a bit, and the number of candles never got higher than 7 to any nation. The only thing that seems to explain this is if 'wandering' candles can be extinguished, and this means that a successful temple check is not guaranteed to produce a candle somewhere.

Now I have a feeling (as it was generally before my time) that this 'guaranteed to produce a candle somewhere' mechanic is what was responsible for the dominion 'jumping' bug that was sometimes seen in games. ie. your dominion would suddenly appear on the other side of the map. ie. Just because a candle is created by a successful temple check, this doesn't mean it HAS to go somewhere.
